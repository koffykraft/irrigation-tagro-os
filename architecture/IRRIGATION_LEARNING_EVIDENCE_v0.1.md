# TAGRO Irrigation OS — Learning Evidence Contract v0.1

Status: WORKING DESIGN

## 1. Purpose

The Irrigation OS should learn from real design and field outcomes without turning habits or correlations into hidden engineering authority.

## 2. Learning event chain

Useful learning evidence follows the lifecycle:

`proposed → accepted/modified/rejected → estimated → purchased → installed → changed → serviced → observed outcome`

Clicks alone are weak evidence.

## 3. Learning record

A learning record may include:

- proposal or design edition reference
- entities involved
- crop / field context
- engineering state at the time
- product candidates considered
- selected product / generic choice
- budget and staging constraints
- user preference signals
- accepted geometry decision
- installed reality
- later modification
- service history
- performance observation
- evidence source
- confidence

## 4. Allowed use

Learning may improve:

- option ranking
- likely next question
- likely starting assumption
- product comparison emphasis
- human-operability suggestions
- future-expansion suggestions
- explanation quality

## 5. Forbidden use

Learning must not silently override:

- deterministic hydraulic calculations
- manufacturer verified limits
- accepted geometry
- explicit user choices
- product evidence provenance
- safety or regulatory constraints

## 6. Rejection is useful evidence

A rejected proposal is not failure data to be discarded. It may reveal:

- access preference
- budget sensitivity
- product preference
- labour tolerance
- aesthetic/land-use concern
- future expansion intent
- local practice

The reason for rejection should be preserved when known.

## 7. Installed outcome is stronger than accepted intent

Accepted design reflects intention. Installed design reflects reality. Service and performance history may reveal whether the installed choice was practical.

The system should distinguish these states instead of flattening them into one design record.

## 8. Governing sentence

**Learn from what was proposed, chosen, installed and later experienced; use that evidence to improve future attention and explanation, but never let repetition become invisible engineering truth.**
